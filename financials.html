6.0 Financial Plan

Complete section text and full table values from the business plan.

MH will be a 501(C)3 organization and will rely on grant funding to establish the care center. The organization is seeking $700,000 to initiate the renovation of the flagship location and to help with startup costs for the first twelve months of operation. The business model helps cover care costs for those who cannot afford it, so regular funding will be necessary to supplement resident costs.

The first six months of operation will be used to renovate the flagship operation, hire staff, test operational systems and select MH residents. The home will function between 50-60% capacity for the first four months after the official opening. By the end of the first year, it should be at least at 90% capacity. The owners have planned to select, retrofit, and open a second MH Location in the city by the 4th year of operation. The tables and charts that follow provide financial projections over the next three years.

6.1 Projected Cash Flow

MH is a non-profit organization created to provide veterans and those in need with quality housing, care, preventative healthcare, and an active lifestyle. The center will rely on funding through grants, charities, and financial contributions to supplement operating costs.

The cost for health care and labor is considerably high and will amount to the majority of the operational costs. Services will not be free, but with adequate initial funding MH will function slightly within the red for only the first three years of operation. Fourth-year projections, adding in the opening of the second care home will pull Madison House out of the red and allow it to generate at a modest profit. With the acquisition of other facilities, profit margin will grow substantially.
